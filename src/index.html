Our FS Project
  1. Aerodynamics We design the Formula Student aero package (front wing, rear diffuser and bodywork) using computational fluid dynamics (CFD), which simulates the airflow. We iterate aerofoil geometry and analyse downforce and drag across configurations.
  2. Chassis A chromoly steel (Cr-Mo) space frame, designed with finite element analysis (FEA) for torsional stiffness and side-impact resistance under the Formula Student Germany (FSG) rulebook, plus the double-wishbone suspension that goes on it.
  3. Powertrain We choose the engine, gearbox and cooling system, weighing each option by mass, reliability, parts availability and the rulebook, including the 20 mm air-intake restrictor.
  4. Electronics Sensors, the CAN bus (the car's data network) and our own telemetry board, along with the software that reads and displays the data.

The Team

We're high school students at Uniwersyteckie Liceum Ogólnokształcące im. Pawła Adamowicza in Gdańsk (ULO). About 50 people have been part of the team so far, and around 25 are on it now. We meet once a week, because our school timetables won't allow more, so most of the work happens at home in the evenings.

Pods

We work in five pods: aero, chassis, suspension, powertrain and electrical. Each has a lead, and our project manager holds it together and talks to companies. Some of us carry more than one pod. That's not how it's meant to work, but it's how it works for now.

We teach ourselves

Nobody shows up already knowing this. Everyone learns CAD, and whoever runs simulations learns CFD and FEA on top. We send each other tutorials and explain things to each other: if someone's work is unclear, you ask and they walk you through it.

What's hard

Time and communication. Getting 25 people with different school timetables working on the right things at once is hard, and we don't always manage it. Sometimes people sit idle because they don't know what to do, and that's our problem to fix. We'd also rather get things right than fast: sometimes we pick a worse part because it's more reliable, and that care takes time.

We started over

Our first year mostly went to waste. When it ended, the teacher running the club left the school, and that's when we saw that most of what we thought was finished wasn't. We started again from zero.

What's next

We want to finish the design by the end of 2026, start building in early 2027 and have the car built by late 2027. That date has moved before, but we believe we can do it. Building takes money, and nobody funds a car before seeing the work. That's why we show ours.
